The tree of life keeps inventing the same tricks

Convergent evolution is what happens when distant branches arrive at the same solution — wings, eyes, bodies of many cells — without sharing the trait in a common ancestor.

By Mark Belan and Hannah Waters September 16, 2026

Life is one tree, not a ladder. Most traits travel with ancestry. A few problems are so useful that separate lineages solve them on their own. That echo — the same idea, written onto far-apart twigs — is convergent evolution.

Below, ten cases. Each one a problem solved twice, on branches that do not share the trait. The last shared organism sits at the fork. The two lineages show how they invented the same answer on their own.
